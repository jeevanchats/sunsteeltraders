## Sample 1: Order-by dates: how to catch material shortages weeks before the build (Blog intro plus outline)

**Intro**

The build starts Monday. On Friday, someone finds the connectors haven't come in. Sound familiar?

In made-to-order work, a shortage rarely shows up early. It shows up on the shop floor, when the job is already late. More chasing won't fix that. What fixes it is one date for every material: the order-by date.

Here's the formula:

**Order-by date = needed-by date − lead time − buffer**

Say a part is needed on the floor on 30 June. Your vendor takes 21 days. You keep 7 days of buffer. Your order-by date is 2 June. If you miss that date, you know the build is at risk today, not on 30 June.

The maths is simple. The hard part is doing it for every material, on every order, every time a date moves. That's what this post is about.

**Outline**

- **Why shortages show up too late**
  - The customer PO date is fixed, but material dates often aren't tracked
  - Spreadsheets and memory stop working after a few live orders
- **The order-by formula, step by step**
  - Needed-by: work back from the build date, which works back from the PO date
  - Lead time: what your vendor actually takes, not what they quote
  - Buffer: how much to keep, and when to keep more
- **Multi-level BOMs: where it gets tricky**
  - Sub-assemblies have their own needed-by dates
  - One late part deep in the BOM can hold up the whole order
- **Job work at vendors**
  - Add the vendor's turnaround to the chain
- **Doing it without the spreadsheet**
  - How WavOS flags late items long before the build starts
  - Bring in items and opening stock from Excel or Tally in a day
